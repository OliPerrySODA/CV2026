<a id="how-i-think-about-recruitment"></a>

<details open>
  <summary><strong>How I think about recruitment</strong></summary>

<br>

(Would need 20,000 words to cover everything I think about recruitment, but below are some of my core beliefs)

<br><br>

- L&D – Training is #1 capital investment, a good recruitment business as a world class L&D operation. This is not just a dedicated L&D professional, but how the leadership team think and behave. The best recruitment leaders are world class coaches and have the skill to provide L&D that improves everyone around them.

<br>

- KPIs – Firm believer in you can’t improve what you can’t measure. I believe in a system where at the click of a button you can track every data point possible. The skill is in how you focus on the right data at the right time. The pinnacle is where you get to focus on outcomes (not just inputs). Although most teams & recruiters need inputs measured, tracked and someone holding them accountable to delivering “What good looks like” on a weekly basis. (CV to Interview is my favourite ratio)

<br>

- Communication – Be clear about priorities. Most anxiety is a difficult conversation you haven’t and yet and in this job you are having to have a lot of difficult conversations. Always best for you and everyone around you if these happen as quickly as possible. Being clear is kind, giving everyone clarity on what you are thinking reduces in-decision and drives focus from everyone around you.

<br>

- AI & Automation – I think recruitment will be just fine, but I do think recruitment leaders can increase NFI per head performance and drive efficiency. Creating systems that save time is the first place to start. Then if you have all your data well organised, there is opportunity to drive lead systems that help increase quality business development workflows.

<br>

- Community & Entertainment – The best form of business development is meeting people face to face. The best way to do this is build offline communities and to take your customers & candidates out for coffee, dinner and wine. The best offline communities right now are intimate roundtables led by a subject matter expert.

<br>

- Quantity vs Quality – I think quantity leads to quality. Recruiters who “do less” on the belief they are doing “quality work” are usually saying this as smokescreen. If you want to become better at sales calls, make more calls.

<br>

- Personal Brand – It should be a key KPI, with the rise of AI and automation I believe Personal Brand is the differentiator. Not just for recruiters to become famous in their niche, but for leaders in how they attract talent. Content is king and in a low trust world, building trust on scale through personal brand is important.

<br>

- Strategy vs Execution – I believe in 360 recruitment and especially recruiters owning a niche. The key strategy is what you do when you start (market mapping, key CAPEX problems, personas etc), but 90% of success is then in execution.

<br>

- Leadership – As a leader, everything is your fault, the good and the bad. You either hired wrong, poorly managed people or didn’t exit quick enough if things go badly. The 4 pillars of leadership I bought in to Trinnovo were Humility (it’s not all about you), Customers (no hiding in the shadows), Coach (don’t just ask people to do more, make then better), Lead (do everything you ask of others). Leading with fear is lazy, better to lead with purpose and have people enjoy working for you than just managing through fear.

<br>

- Toxicity – If someone in a recruitment business is proactively toxic (aka culture detractor) and purposefully pulling everything down around them, they need to go, regardless of tenure or billings.

<br>

- Goal Setting – Winners and losers have the same goal. You fall to the level of your systems. I am a believer in people showing me how they are going to achieve their goals rather than just telling me their goals. How they set up for the day, their recruitment process, how they follow up, templates etc.

<br>

- Hard work – Nothing else matters if you don’t work hard and have discipline.

<br>

- Sales – Sales is complicated and there is a lot to it, but I think the lowest hanging fruit that recruitment gets wrong is story telling. We talk too much about ourselves and not enough about the customer. Story telling is creating emotion and having enough EQ to position it correctly for your customers. I also believe in the importance of a “Sales Floor” and that outbound sales calls still play a big part in the success of a 360 recruiter. I also think people underestimate the importance of world class customer service & just rely on filling jobs,
